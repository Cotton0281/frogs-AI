A colony of digital creatures lives inside a population. 

Each creature has a brain recipe, a score, and a chance to become one of the “best genes” in the group. The app watches them, ranks them by fitness, and shows the results in UI grids so the human operator can play the role of dramatic reality-show host: “And this week’s least effective frog brain has been eliminated.”

In practical terms, the project appears to:

•	manage a population of AI-controlled agents,

•	track members and their best genome records, 

•	score them with a fitness value, 

•	sort the strongest candidates to the top,

•	display the current population in a desktop UI, 

•	and refresh that view as evolution continues.

So the story version is: 

A bunch of robot frogs enter a survival tournament.

The clever ones get promoted.

The bad thinkers get quietly judged by a DataGridView.

The whole population keeps mutating, competing, and being ranked until someone becomes the frog equivalent of a tactical genius.

It is essentially an AI evolution simulator with a desktop viewer—part laboratory, part leaderboard, part amphibian talent show.
